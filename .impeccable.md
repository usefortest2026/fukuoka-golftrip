## Design Context

### Users

This is a private Fukuoka golf-trip companion for a small group of friends. They use it mainly on phones while moving between the airport, hotels, golf courses, restaurants, and shops. The interface should make the day's timing, navigation, booking details, coupons, and occasional group decisions immediately usable with one hand and limited attention.

### Brand Personality

Calm, practical, and quietly premium. It should feel like a well-prepared personal travel booklet made for this group, not a generic travel product, promotional landing page, settings dashboard, or arcade game. Playful moments are welcome only when they remain restrained and connected to the golf-trip context.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a light, editorial travel-booklet language with forest green, warm paper neutrals, fine rules, crisp typography, and scorecard-inspired details. Prefer flat grouping, alignment, and spacing over stacked cards, large icon tiles, gradients, or conspicuous shadows. Interactive elements should feel native to the itinerary and remain compact without sacrificing 44px touch targets or accessibility.

### Design Principles

1. The itinerary is always the visual anchor; utilities stay subordinate to trip content.
2. Use cards only for genuinely distinct content. Prefer dividers, columns, and whitespace; never create card-on-card layouts.
3. Golf references should feel like a real scorecard or clubhouse detail, not cartoon equipment or casino imagery.
4. Keep one obvious action per moment, with concise bilingual copy and visible status feedback.
5. Design mobile-first for distracted, one-handed use; preserve keyboard, screen-reader, reduced-motion, and offline behavior.
